Coverage Indications, Limitations, and/or Medical Necessity

Covered Indications

Ultomiris (ravulizumab-cwvz, BLA 761068) is covered by CGS Administrators LLC for the treatment of adults with atypical Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (aHUS) who meet ALL of the following criteria:

  1. Confirmed diagnosis of complement-mediated thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA) consistent with aHUS, as evidenced by microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, and acute kidney injury in the absence of a primary cause such as Shiga toxin-producing E. coli (STEC-HUS) or thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP);
  2. Laboratory confirmation of complement pathway activation (low C3, elevated sC5b-9, or identified pathogenic variant in a complement-regulatory gene); and
  3. Evaluation by or in consultation with a specialist in hematology, nephrology, or rare disease medicine.

Coverage Limitations

Coverage for Ravulizumab is subject to the following limitations:

Limitation 1 — Step Therapy Requirement

Prior to initiation of Ravulizumab, beneficiaries must have a documented clinical trial of plasma exchange (PE) therapy. Specifically, coverage requires documentation of:

  • At least two (2) courses of plasma exchange therapy administered within the 12-month period preceding the proposed initiation of Ravulizumab; AND
  • Clinical failure, defined as persistence of TMA laboratory abnormalities (platelet count <150,000/µL, LDH >1.5× upper limit of normal, or ongoing renal injury) following completion of each PE course.

CGS Rationale: Plasma exchange remains the standard of care for initial management of thrombotic microangiopathy and represents adequate first-line therapy for the majority of patients presenting with complement-mediated TMA. The high cost and irreversibility of complement inhibitor therapy warrants demonstration that conventional management has been attempted prior to initiating biologic therapy.

Limitation 2 — Site of Care Restriction: Inpatient-Only Initial Administration

The initial dose and, at minimum, the first two (2) subsequent doses of Ravulizumab must be administered in an inpatient hospital setting. The facility must demonstrate capacity for:

  • Immediate management of severe infusion-related or hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis; AND
  • Rapid clinical response to suspected or confirmed meningococcal infection, including access to appropriate antibiotics, blood cultures, and intensive care support.

Outpatient administration of subsequent doses (dose 4 and beyond) is permitted provided the treating physician documents that the patient tolerated inpatient initiation without grade 3 or higher adverse events.

CGS Rationale: The FDA-approved prescribing information for Ravulizumab (Ultomiris) includes a Boxed Warning identifying meningococcal infection as a serious, life-threatening risk associated with complement inhibition. This risk is a class-wide concern for C5 inhibitors. CGS Administrators requires inpatient initiation to ensure that patients have access to immediate clinical intervention if meningococcal sepsis or other serious infections emerge during the period of greatest immunologic vulnerability following treatment initiation.

Limitation 3 — Duration of Coverage: 12-Week Initial Authorization Period

Initial coverage authorization for Ravulizumab is limited to twelve (12) weeks from the date of first administration. Prior to the expiration of the initial authorization period, the treating physician must submit documentation for continued coverage authorization including:

  • Laboratory evidence of sustained TMA response at week 12, defined as platelet count ≥150,000/µL and LDH within normal limits;
  • Evidence of renal function stability or improvement (serum creatinine at or below baseline); AND
  • Attestation that the patient remains dialysis-free.

Continued coverage beyond 12 weeks will be evaluated on a case-by-case basis. Coverage will not be authorized for patients who have not demonstrated sustained TMA response by week 12.

CGS Rationale: Published evidence supporting the long-term benefit of complement inhibition beyond 12 weeks in the outpatient aHUS population is derived from single-arm observational studies and registry data, which are subject to selection bias and lack comparator arms. CGS finds that the clinical benefit of indefinite complement inhibition beyond the initial treatment period has not been established to the standard required for unrestricted coverage under the Medicare benefit. A 12-week authorization period with re-evaluation is consistent with the evidentiary limitations of the available literature.

Summary of Evidence

Overview of Evidence Reviewed

CGS Administrators has reviewed the available clinical evidence for Ultomiris (ravulizumab-cwvz, BLA 761068) in the treatment of atypical hemolytic uremic syndrome (aHUS), including the pivotal clinical trial submission to the FDA, the approved prescribing information, post-marketing registry data submitted by the manufacturer, published peer-reviewed literature for the C5 complement inhibitor class, and analogous Local Coverage Determinations issued by other Medicare Administrative Contractors. The evidence reviewed informed the covered indication and the limitations described herein. CGS acknowledges that complement inhibition represents an important therapeutic advance for patients with complement-mediated TMA; however, the specificity of covered criteria and the limitations described in this LCD reflect CGS's determination that the available evidence does not yet support unrestricted outpatient initiation or indefinite coverage without reassessment.

Disease Natural History and Unmet Need

Atypical hemolytic uremic syndrome is a rare, life-threatening complement-mediated thrombotic microangiopathy characterized by microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, and acute kidney injury. Unlike STEC-HUS, aHUS is driven by dysregulation of the alternative complement pathway due to inherited or acquired abnormalities in complement regulatory proteins, including factor H, factor I, membrane cofactor protein (MCP/CD46), C3, factor B, and thrombomodulin. Without effective complement inhibition, aHUS carries a risk of end-stage renal disease exceeding 50% within the first year of a presenting episode, and relapse upon discontinuation of treatment is well-documented in the class literature. CGS acknowledges the severity of untreated aHUS and the clinical rationale for targeted complement inhibition.

Pivotal Trial Evidence — Ravulizumab (BLA 761068)

The pivotal trial for Ravulizumab in aHUS (NCT03131219, ALXN1210-aHUS-311) enrolled adult patients with aHUS naive to complement inhibitor treatment. The study reported a primary endpoint of complete thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA) response at 26 weeks, defined as normalization of platelet count (≥150,000/µL), LDH within normal limits, and serum creatinine at or below baseline. The trial population was heterogeneous with respect to prior treatment history; it included patients who had received prior plasma exchange therapy as well as patients initiating complement inhibition as first-line therapy. Initiation settings also varied: patients were enrolled in both inpatient and outpatient settings per site practice. The trial was single-arm and lacked a concurrent comparator group. CGS finds that the absence of a randomized, controlled comparator limits the ability to quantify the marginal benefit of Ravulizumab over standard supportive care — including plasma exchange and best supportive care — in the general aHUS population presenting to Medicare-enrolled providers.

Class Analogue Evidence — Eculizumab

The class evidence base for terminal complement inhibition in aHUS is primarily derived from eculizumab (Soliris, Alexion/AstraZeneca), the first approved C5 inhibitor for this indication. Published pivotal and extension studies for eculizumab in aHUS (Legendre et al., NEJM 2013; Licht et al., Kidney Int 2015) demonstrated sustained TMA response and renal function preservation over 2-year follow-up periods in predominantly treatment-naïve or plasma-exchange-refractory patients. CGS notes that these studies were also single-arm and conducted in highly selected populations at academic or specialty referral centers. CGS has reviewed published class safety data including the eculizumab renal registry (Rondeau et al., Kidney Int Rep 2019), which enrolled patients across a range of practice settings, and notes that meningococcal infection events have occurred in post-marketing experience despite low reported incidence in controlled trials.

Step Therapy and Plasma Exchange

Plasma exchange (PE) has historically been used as initial management for thrombotic microangiopathy because it can acutely remove anti-complement antibodies and replenish depleted regulatory factors. CGS acknowledges that the 2017 KDIGO Controversies Conference report (Goodship et al., Kidney Int 2017) noted that PE is no longer recommended as first-line therapy in confirmed complement-mediated aHUS and that immediate complement inhibition is preferred where the diagnosis is clear. However, CGS finds that in clinical practice, the diagnosis of complement-mediated aHUS is frequently uncertain at the time of presentation, and plasma exchange may be appropriate as a bridging therapy while genetic and serological workup is pending. CGS further notes that Medicare beneficiaries presenting with de novo TMA may receive an initial plasma exchange course as standard hospital practice before a confirmed aHUS diagnosis and complement inhibitor initiation. The step-therapy requirement in this LCD is calibrated to require documentation of an attempted conventional therapeutic approach and of clinical failure prior to initiating a high-cost biologic, not to mandate plasma exchange in patients for whom the diagnosis has already been confirmed by complement studies or genetic testing.

Site-of-Care Considerations

The FDA-approved prescribing information for Ravulizumab (Ultomiris) carries a Boxed Warning identifying life-threatening and fatal meningococcal infections as a class risk for all complement inhibitors. This risk is a pharmacological consequence of terminal complement pathway inhibition, which impairs opsonization of Neisseria meningitidis. The Boxed Warning requires meningococcal vaccination prior to treatment initiation and recommends monitoring for signs of infection. CGS recognizes that meningococcal infections are rare in the clinical trial setting; however, the potential severity of meningococcal sepsis and meningitis — which can progress from symptom onset to death within 24 hours — warrants a conservative approach to the initiation phase of therapy. Inpatient initiation of the first three doses ensures that patients have access to blood cultures, empirical antibiotic therapy, and intensive care support during the period of highest immunological vulnerability. CGS will consider modification of the site-of-care requirement as post-marketing safety data for Ravulizumab specifically accumulates in the outpatient setting.

Duration of Coverage and Long-Term Benefit

Published data on the long-term benefit of complement inhibition in aHUS beyond 12 weeks are derived from single-arm observational studies and registry data, which are subject to selection bias and lack comparator arms. Data from the eculizumab post-marketing registry demonstrate that a significant proportion of patients relapse upon treatment discontinuation, supporting the need for ongoing therapy in many patients. However, a subset of patients — particularly those with anti-factor H antibodies or certain complement pathway mutations — may tolerate treatment discontinuation under close monitoring. CGS finds that the clinical benefit of indefinite complement inhibition without reassessment has not been established to the standard required for unrestricted long-term coverage under the Medicare benefit. The 12-week authorization period with clinical reassessment is intended to confirm sustained TMA response and continued medical necessity, not to create a presumption against continued coverage for responding patients.

CGS Coverage Committee Determination

Based on the totality of evidence reviewed, the CGS Coverage Committee finds that Ravulizumab is covered for adult beneficiaries with confirmed complement-mediated TMA consistent with aHUS who meet the criteria set forth above. The coverage limitations reflect the Committee's determination that the available evidence supports cautious, monitored initiation with step-therapy documentation, inpatient oversight during the initiation period, and reassessment at 12 weeks to confirm sustained benefit. These limitations are subject to revision as post-marketing evidence for Ravulizumab accumulates and as the standard of care for aHUS continues to evolve.

Documentation Requirements

All of the following documentation must be present in the medical record and available for review upon request or at the time of prior authorization submission:

  1. Signed ordering physician attestation confirming diagnosis of complement-mediated TMA consistent with aHUS, including explicit exclusion of STEC-HUS and TTP as alternative diagnoses;
  2. Laboratory results within 30 days of initiation documenting microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (elevated LDH, peripheral smear with schistocytes, direct Coombs negative or indeterminate), thrombocytopenia (platelet count <150,000/µL), and acute kidney injury;
  3. Evidence of complement pathway activation: at minimum one of (a) low C3, (b) elevated sC5b-9 (soluble terminal complement complex), or (c) identified pathogenic variant in a complement-regulatory gene (CFH, CFI, MCP/CD46, C3, CFB, THBD, DGKE) documented by a CLIA-certified laboratory;
  4. Documentation of prior plasma exchange therapy including: dates of administration, number of sessions per course, facility or provider of record, and laboratory values (platelet count, LDH, serum creatinine) following each course demonstrating clinical failure as defined in Limitation 1;
  5. Meningococcal vaccination documentation: confirmation that the patient has received meningococcal vaccines covering serogroups A, C, W, Y and B (MenACWY and MenB) at least 2 weeks prior to initiation, or documentation of clinical urgency requiring initiation before vaccination could be completed with a concurrent prophylactic antibiotic prescription on file;
  6. Specialist attestation: a signed note from a hematologist, nephrologist, or rare disease medicine specialist confirming that the diagnosis, treatment plan, and ongoing monitoring protocol have been reviewed and are consistent with current clinical standards;
  7. For re-authorization submissions (beyond 12 weeks): laboratory values dated within 14 days of the week-12 anniversary demonstrating sustained TMA response (platelet count ≥150,000/µL, LDH within normal limits, serum creatinine at or below baseline), and a physician attestation that the patient remains dialysis-free.

Contractor Advisory Committee (CAC) Meeting Notes

A Contractor Advisory Committee meeting was convened on March 14, 2026 to gather expert clinical input prior to drafting this LCD. The following summary reflects consensus and dissenting views of the advisory panel:

  • Step Therapy (Limitation 1): Panel members were divided. Three of five clinical advisors noted that current clinical practice guidelines (KDIGO 2017) no longer recommend plasma exchange as first-line therapy for complement-mediated TMA, and that delays in complement inhibition may cause irreversible renal injury. Two advisors supported a limited trial of PE in patients without immediately life-threatening presentation.
  • Site of Care (Limitation 2): All five panel members agreed that the meningococcal infection risk warrants caution during the initial treatment period, though opinions differed on whether full inpatient admission is necessary versus monitored outpatient infusion in a hospital-based infusion center with emergency response capability.
  • Duration Limit (Limitation 3): Panel members noted that published real-world data for the C5 inhibitor class (eculizumab, ravulizumab) demonstrates that a significant proportion of patients require indefinite therapy due to the risk of relapse upon discontinuation. The panel recommended that CGS reconsider a strict 12-week limit in favor of a clinical re-evaluation requirement at 12 weeks that does not automatically terminate coverage.

The CGS Coverage Committee considered these recommendations in drafting this LCD. The final limitations reflect the committee's determination of appropriate coverage policy based on the totality of evidence reviewed, including the CAC input received.
